# GEO measurement and evaluation source notes

**Research date:** 2026-08-30

## Google Search Console

Google describes Search Console as a tool for understanding how a site performs in Google Search and what can be improved. Its getting-started guidance says owners should verify ownership, make sure Google can find and read pages, consider submitting a sitemap, and monitor the Search performance report. The Performance report includes trends for impressions, clicks, queries, pages, countries, and related metrics. Google also points owners to Index Coverage, URL Inspection, Rich result status reports, Security Issues, and Core Web Vitals for different classes of diagnosis.

Source: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-console-start

## Structured data validation

Google recommends the Rich Results Test for checking which Google rich results can be generated from structured data, and points to the Schema Markup Validator for generic Schema.org validation. Structured data can support interpretation and eligible search appearances; it is not a claim-verification mechanism.

Source: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data

## NIST AI RMF

NIST describes the AI Risk Management Framework as a voluntary framework intended to improve the ability to incorporate trustworthiness considerations into the design, development, use, and evaluation of AI products, services, and systems. The AI RMF Core is organized around Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage. NIST states that the functions are iterative and that the actions are not necessarily an ordered checklist.

Sources: https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework and https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/

## Coreweaver interpretation

The next high-leverage learning gap is measurement and evaluation. The current AI Mastery graph covers entity records, structured relationships, discovery files, trust, interoperability, commerce, governance, and GEO implementation. A dedicated measurement silo can teach learners how to distinguish structural checks (HTTP, links, canonicals, robots, sitemap, schema) from external outcome measures (impressions, clicks, citations, retrieval, conversions, and authority metrics). It should not claim that technical compliance produces ranking or answer inclusion.
