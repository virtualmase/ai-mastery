# Section 08 content brief: Economic Models and Agentic Commerce

## Reader question

How does value move through an agentic commerce workflow, and where do authorization, settlement, fulfillment, reconciliation, and dispute responsibilities actually sit?

## Intended audience

Technical founders, product engineers, operators, and researchers designing or reviewing software that can discover offers, request work, authorize spend, exchange value, or coordinate delivery across systems.

## Evidence boundary

This is educational systems material. It does not provide investment, payment, tax, legal, accounting, or consumer-finance advice. It does not recommend a token, wallet, provider, commercial model, or transaction. It does not claim that an agent can transact safely, that a protocol guarantees settlement, that a payment authorization proves delivery, or that any model is profitable or likely to be adopted.

## Four-lesson sequence

1. **Value, price, payment, settlement, and fulfillment** — distinguish commercial stages and the evidence each stage can actually establish.
2. **Mandates, spend limits, and policy** — represent principal, agent, scope, amount, counterparty, expiry, revocation, and approval without confusing a technical credential with consent.
3. **Reconciliation, disputes, and exception economics** — model ledger entries, idempotency, refunds, chargebacks, partial delivery, and unresolved state as operating work rather than edge cases.
4. **Incentives, fees, and risk allocation** — map who pays, who benefits, who bears failure, what the fee buys, and which externalities remain unknown without forecasting profitability.
