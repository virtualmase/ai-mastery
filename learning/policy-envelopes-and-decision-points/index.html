Foundation 02 · Policy envelopes

Make the
boundary executable.

Policy becomes useful when it names the input, the decision, the conditions, the evidence, and the enforcement point. A vague intention is not a control, and a policy engine’s answer is not proof that the input was sufficient.

STARTING QUESTIONWhat may happen, under which conditions, with what evidence and what safe hold?

Model scope, allow, deny, hold, expiry, revocation, and enforcement separately.

Learning boundary. This lesson is a policy-modeling exercise, not legal advice, compliance approval, security certification, or authorization to operate an autonomous workflow. A policy decision is not enforcement, and enforcement is not proof that the policy was appropriate.

Learning objective

Translate intent into inspectable decisions.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to write the inputs, conditions, outputs, evidence, expiry, and enforcement point for a bounded workflow policy.

01 · Scope

What is covered?

Name the subject, resource, operation, counterparty, context, and time window. A policy cannot govern what it does not identify.

02 · Condition

What must be true?

Specify required evidence, thresholds, approvals, attributes, and exclusions. Missing input should produce an explicit unknown or hold.

03 · Decision

What is the output?

Keep allow, deny, hold, escalate, and review-needed states distinct. A binary answer can hide the work still required.

04 · Enforcement

Where does it stop?

Name the component that blocks, pauses, routes, or records the decision. A policy engine cannot enforce a result unless the caller honors it.

Practice lab

Write the envelope.

A workflow may draft a purchase order for a known supplier. It may not submit the order if the amount exceeds a threshold, supplier evidence is stale, or the request falls outside the principal’s mandate.

  1. Write the subject, operation, resource, supplier, amount, currency, and time window.
  2. Define the evidence required for an allow decision and the conditions that produce hold.
  3. Name the enforcement point and what record is written for each result.
  4. Define expiry, revocation, and the safe behavior when a policy input is missing.

Answer direction

Make “no” useful.

A good envelope does not only permit or reject. It tells the caller what is missing, where the decision applies, when it expires, who can revise it, and how a human can review the hold.

Source shelf

Study governance and policy evaluation.

  1. Open Policy Agent documentationStudy the separation between policy decision-making and enforcement, structured input, declarative rules, and structured output. An OPA result does not prove that input or policy is complete.
  2. NIST AI Risk Management FrameworkUse Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage as a risk-management vocabulary; it is voluntary guidance, not a local authorization decision.
