Foundation 01 · Evidence boundaries

Inspect the claim
before the signal.

A badge, signature, log, citation, or credential is never the first question. Start by naming the claim, the evidence that could bear on it, the scope of that evidence, and what remains unknown.

STARTING QUESTIONWhat exactly is being asserted, by whom, about what?

Only then ask whether the evidence is attributable, current, complete enough for the decision, and open to correction.

Learning boundary. This lesson provides an inspection framework. It does not determine the truth of any claim, validate a credential, certify a source, assess compliance, or make a reliance decision for a reader.

Learning objective

Separate the statement from the reason to rely on it.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to distinguish a claim, its issuer, direct evidence, interpretive context, scope, unknowns, and the different decision a particular reader might make.

01 · Claim

What is being said?

Write a bounded statement about a named subject. Avoid folding the claim, the conclusion, and a marketing adjective into the same sentence.

02 · Evidence

What observation, record, or method bears on it?

Link the claim to a source that can be inspected. Label whether it is direct evidence, an interpretation, a framework, or an unresolved assertion.

03 · Scope

What does the evidence not establish?

Name the time range, audience, conditions, and missing facts. A source can be authentic and still be too narrow or old for the proposed decision.

04 · Reliance

Who decides whether it is enough?

A verifier or reader applies their own rule, context, and threshold. Technical verification and business, safety, legal, or editorial judgment are separate steps.

Practice lab

Unpack a product statement before sharing it.

A public page says a workflow is “verified, secure, and reliable.” It links to a screenshot of a green status panel and a dated engineering note, but does not name the system boundary, test method, owner, or conditions under which the statement applies.

  1. Rewrite the claim as three separable, testable statements.
  2. List the evidence a reader could inspect for each statement.
  3. Write the scope and unknowns that the screenshot and note leave open.
  4. Name the decision a reader must still make rather than treating the display as a conclusion.

Answer direction

Evidence is not a verdict.

A panel may record a status event; it does not establish a broad security or reliability claim. A dated note may explain a method but not prove current conditions. Keep each claim narrow, attach the relevant record, expose uncertainty, and let the intended reviewer apply an explicit decision rule.
