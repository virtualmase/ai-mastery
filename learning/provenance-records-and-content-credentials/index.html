Foundation 02 · Provenance records

Provenance can show
a relationship.

A provenance record can help a reader inspect how an asset relates to its assertions, signers, bindings, and changes. It does not turn a weak statement into a strong one or replace the reader’s judgment.

STARTING QUESTIONWhat record binds this assertion to this asset and signer?

Trace the relationship, then examine what the record validates and what the underlying claim still requires.

Learning boundary. This lesson explains a provenance-inspection method. It does not validate a C2PA manifest, authenticate a specific file, determine author identity, establish truth, or provide an implementation or security assessment.

Learning objective

Read a provenance record as evidence about a record—not as a universal trust score.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to identify the asset, claim, signer, content binding, assertions, validation state, and missing context that a provenance record makes available to a reader.

01 · Asset

Which artifact is the record about?

Identify the exact file, stream, or referenced asset. Ask how the claimed asset is bound to the record and what may happen if metadata is removed, altered, or separated.

02 · Claim

Who signed which assertions?

Separate a signer’s assertion from independent evidence about the assertion. A signature can make the provenance relationship inspectable without establishing the world described by the claim.

03 · Validation

What did a validator actually check?

Read the status, cryptographic checks, and disclosed failures. Do not compress a technical validation result into a broad “real,” “safe,” or “true” label.

04 · Context

What remains outside the manifest?

Assess source reputation, editorial context, claimed event, completeness, recency, and the reader’s intended use. These judgments sit outside a content-binding check.

Practice lab

Inspect a signed media claim without overreading it.

A news image has an attached provenance record listing a creator application, a signer, two edit actions, and a valid content binding. A social post says the record “proves the image is true.” The event shown is not independently described in the record.

  1. List what the record can help a reader inspect about the image and its claim history.
  2. Identify the difference between a valid binding and the truth of the depicted event.
  3. Write the additional reporting, source, or corroboration questions needed for an editorial decision.
  4. Name the appropriate correction route if the caption or contextual claim is later shown to be wrong.

Answer direction

Verify the record; evaluate the claim.

The record may help establish that the displayed manifest is bound to the asset and was signed by a stated identity under the validator’s trust model. It does not, on its own, prove the event, caption, intent, or completeness. A responsible editor still evaluates the provenance, surrounding evidence, and correction process.

Source shelf

Read the technical boundary in the specification.

  1. C2PA Technical Specification 2.4Inspect the specification’s terminology for assets, assertions, claims, content bindings, manifests, validation, and trust signals. Its guiding scope cautions against making value judgments from validated provenance data alone.
  2. C2PA guidance for AI and MLUse this guidance to ask what an AI-related assertion and its provenance record disclose. It is not a statement that a labelled asset is true, lawful, high quality, or appropriate for a specific use.
