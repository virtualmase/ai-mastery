OPERATING
POSITION

Capability without a boundary is not autonomy.
It is unowned exposure.

The purpose of Autonomous Resource Management is not to make every workflow act alone. It is to make every delegation legible enough that another accountable person can understand what was permitted, what occurred, and what must happen next.

LEARNING BOUNDARY

This pathway teaches an operating model. It does not certify a system, promise a model can govern itself, or replace the human responsibility attached to a consequential decision.

01

DECISION
RIGHTS ATLAS

Four checks before
an action can travel.

Read from left to right. Each stage creates a constraint on the next, so a workflow cannot treat available capability as implied authority.

STAGE01

RESOURCE INVENTORY

What can this workflow consume, alter, disclose, or commit?

List the data, tools, time, funds, people, and downstream dependencies before calling an action “safe.”
  • Scope the resource
  • Name the owner
  • Set the limit
STAGE02

DECISION RIGHTS

What may it recommend, execute, approve, or never touch?

Separate analysis from action. A recommendation can travel further than an execution right; an exception requires a named accountable decision.
  • Recommend
  • Execute within limit
  • Escalate exception
STAGE03

TRACE RECORD

Could a later reviewer reconstruct the decision without guessing?

Keep the source, instruction, candidate action, authorization, final action, outcome, and unresolved uncertainty together.
  • Evidence attached
  • Decision attributable
  • Outcome reviewable
STAGE04

HUMAN ESCALATION

What signal pauses the workflow, and who resolves it?

Predefine the conditions that stop automation: scope breach, uncertainty, conflict, policy exception, or a result that changes another person’s rights.
  • Monitor the limit
  • Hold on breach
  • Route accountable decision

RECONSTRUCTABILITY TEST

Can a new reviewer answer five questions?

Not “does the log exist?” but whether the log makes responsibility and consequence legible without tribal knowledge.

  • 01What was known at the time?
  • 02Which boundary applied?
  • 03Who had the decision right?
  • 04What action was actually taken?
  • 05What changed after the action?

before the system runs.

A facilities workflow wants to reschedule preventive maintenance when sensor data suggests a potential equipment issue. The change may displace production work, consume technician time, and trigger a supplier commitment.

01

What resources and downstream commitments can this touch?

02

What can it recommend—and what must a person approve?

03

Which facts and alternatives belong in the trace record?

04

Which signals stop the workflow and route an exception?

ANSWER DIRECTION / NOT A PRESCRIPTION

Rank, do not silently commit.

The workflow can identify and rank candidate schedules. A named human should approve a production-critical displacement, labor or budget threshold breach, uncertain sensor evidence, or any commitment that changes another team’s rights. Preserve the sensor evidence, recommendation, alternatives, approval, action, and observed outcome.
