Foundation 03 · Measurement and change

Measure the
change.

A workflow can remain within a written policy while its data, dependencies, prompts, models, traffic, or effects change around it. Governance needs a measurement question, a review cadence, a release record, and a person who can pause or roll back the change.

STARTING QUESTIONWhat changed, how would we know, and who can pause the new state?

Connect risk hypothesis, measure, threshold, owner, release record, and rollback authority.

Learning boundary. This lesson is an evaluation and change-control exercise, not a performance guarantee, safety assessment, compliance finding, or prediction about a system’s future behavior. A metric can reveal a signal without proving causality, quality, fairness, or acceptable risk.

Learning objective

Make change reviewable over time.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to connect a governance concern to a measurable signal, a decision threshold, an accountable reviewer, and a controlled release or rollback record.

01 · Hypothesis

What could drift?

State the risk or operating assumption to inspect: data quality, access pattern, model behavior, latency, cost, harm signal, or external dependency.

02 · Measure

What is observable?

Define the metric, sample, baseline, source, frequency, and missing-data behavior. A number without a measurement design is a decoration.

03 · Review

Who decides?

Name the owner, threshold, review window, escalation route, and action for a signal. Avoid letting an alert silently rewrite the policy.

04 · Change

What may move?

Record version, scope, approver, tests, dependencies, rollback condition, and effective date. Separate a release from evidence that it worked.

Practice lab

Build a change record.

A support workflow changes its retrieval source and model version. Its written policy is unchanged, but response latency rises and the review team cannot tell whether the new source has altered escalation patterns.

  1. Write two risk hypotheses and one observable measure for each.
  2. Define baseline, sample, review window, threshold, and data owner.
  3. Name who can hold the release, approve a rollback, and review affected cases.
  4. Separate the change record from the evidence needed to decide whether it improved the workflow.

Answer direction

Measure before narrating.

Use the record to preserve what changed and the evaluation to inspect what followed. Do not convert a clean deployment or a single metric into a claim of safety, quality, or successful governance.
