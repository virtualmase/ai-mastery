Foundation 01 · Roles and accountability

Name the
owner.

A workflow can produce a recommendation, invoke a tool, or trigger a review. None of those capabilities names who owns the system, who may decide, who must provide evidence, or who resolves harm.

STARTING QUESTIONWho is accountable for this decision, its evidence, and its consequences?

Name the role, decision, authority, evidence owner, affected party, and resolver before discussing autonomy.

Learning boundary. This lesson is an organizational modeling exercise, not legal advice, compliance approval, safety certification, or proof that an assigned owner can control every outcome. A named role is not evidence that the role has capacity, authority, or oversight.

Learning objective

Turn a stakeholder list into an accountability map.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to separate system ownership, policy ownership, operation, approval, review, affected-party voice, and exception resolution.

01 · System owner

Who maintains the thing?

Names the accountable organization or role for system scope, lifecycle, dependencies, and retirement. Ownership does not equal omniscience.

02 · Policy owner

Who sets the boundary?

Maintains the policy intent, prohibited actions, thresholds, evidence requirements, and review cadence.

03 · Decision owner

Who may approve?

Holds the authority for a consequential decision or defines when approval must remain with a person or domain system.

04 · Resolver

Who repairs disagreement?

Receives exceptions, preserves the record, assigns the next safe state, and documents the decision rather than hiding the conflict.

Practice lab

Draw the responsibility line.

An agent recommends pausing a supplier account after detecting unusual order behavior. A platform team operates the workflow, a procurement lead owns the supplier relationship, and a compliance team reviews policy exceptions.

  1. Name the system owner, policy owner, decision owner, operator, reviewer, affected party, and resolver.
  2. State which role can recommend, approve, execute, reverse, and communicate the pause.
  3. List the evidence each role must inspect before acting.
  4. Define what remains unknown if the signal is plausible but not verified.

Answer direction

Responsibility is not a title.

Attach each role to a decision and an evidence obligation. If nobody can pause, review, or repair the workflow, the governance map is incomplete regardless of the organization chart.
